# Reference playbook

## General (hooks, pacing, camera, sound, captions, endings that make people rewatch)
*   **Hook pacing:** Mandate 3 to 5 visual cuts or distinct camera movements within the first 5 seconds to establish high momentum and prevent scroll-aways.
*   **Caption styling:** Use dynamic, center-screen or lower-third captions displaying only 1 to 3 words at a time. Use a bold sans-serif font with a thick black outline for maximum contrast against any background.
*   **Audio looping:** Script the final sentence fragment to grammatically and tonally connect directly into the first word of the video, creating a seamless audio loop that artificially inflates retention.
*   **Voiceover pacing:** Maintain a voiceover speed between 150 and 185 WPM. Start at peak energy for the first 3 seconds before settling into the core narrative rhythm.
*   **Literal visualizations:** Never just narrate an abstract concept. Mandate a literal, physical 3D manifestation on screen (e.g., a physical breaker box being switched off to represent losing an abstract emotion, or a common snack morphing into a biological structure).
*   **Sound design mapping:** Assign distinct SFX profiles to positive and negative outcomes (e.g., cheerful dings at higher frequencies for success, heavy low-frequency buzzes for failure) to subconsciously guide the viewer's emotional state.
*   **Visual change frequency:** Ensure a distinct visual action, pop-up graphic, or camera cut occurs at least every 2.5 seconds during standard exposition.
*   **Color coding:** Use distinct, contrasting color palettes (e.g., warm/vibrant vs. cool/muted) to instantly separate different locations, timelines, or opposing concepts without needing verbal explanation.

## Explainer
*   **Counter-intuitive hooks:** Open the first 2 seconds with a spoken "fact" or specific number that directly challenges common knowledge or cultural assumptions to force an immediate scroll-stop.
*   **The "Twist" beat:** Insert a narrative pivot between the 0:10 and 0:18 mark that reframes the viewer's understanding of the premise (e.g., shifting the context from a domestic setting to a clinical/scientific one).
*   **Rapid visual proof:** When making a core claim or comparison, show 2 to 3 rapid-fire visual examples within a 5-second window to build credibility before moving to the deeper explanation.
*   **Second-person framing:** Use "You are [Subject]" in the opening line for historical or biographical explainers to force immediate viewer immersion and raise the personal stakes.
*   **Climax-first structure:** Start the video at the story's highest point of conflict or drama, then use a visual rewind transition around the 0:08 mark to explain the context of how the subject got there.
*   **Numbered backbones:** Structure comparisons or multi-point arguments with clear, on-screen numbering to anchor the viewer's progression through the video.
*   **Data visualization:** Represent massive numbers or scale physically rather than just textually (e.g., filling the screen with hundreds of identical character models to represent a user base or workforce).

## Bizarre true story
*   **Monkey's Paw narrative:** Introduce a scenario in the first 15 seconds that sounds highly desirable or utopian, then spend the remaining runtime slowly revealing the hidden psychological or physical nightmare.
*   **Missing element escalation:** Build dread by introducing a seemingly normal secondary character or element early on, then removing them entirely without explanation around the 0:40 mark.
*   **Atmospheric audio:** Layer subtle, repetitive background Foley (like a ticking clock at 60 BPM or rhythmic mechanical sounds) beneath the music track to convey isolation, boredom, or the agonizing passage of time.
*   **Lighting shifts:** Transition the lighting from flat, even studio lighting in the first 20 seconds to dramatic, directional shadows in the final 15 seconds to visually mirror the story's dark turn.
*   **Ironic payoff:** Conclude the narrative with a twist that highlights the absurdity of the situation (e.g., achieving massive global power but lacking any actual ownership or escape).
*   **Energy drop:** Drop the voiceover energy, pace, and pitch significantly in the final 10 seconds to deliver the bleakest or most serious fact of the story, contrasting sharply with the high-energy hook.

## Gaming lore and facts
*   **Split-screen hooks:** Use a visual split-screen in the first 3 seconds to establish a stark "before and after" or a massive time jump to instantly set the stakes of the timeline.
*   **Visual aging:** Represent the passage of time (e.g., a 10+ year span) by changing the character's environment, clothing, and physical features rather than just displaying on-screen dates.
*   **Continuous music:** Use a single, uninterrupted background track (e.g., 80 BPM lo-fi or ambient) to provide a sense of continuity across long, frustrating chronological timelines.
*   **Pop-up data:** Illustrate secondary facts (like sales numbers, delays, or social media posts) using 2D pop-up graphics that appear with a subtle "pop" SFX without breaking the main scene's visual style.
*   **Consistent transitions:** Mandate a specific, repeating transition effect (like a vertical wipe accompanied by a "whoosh" SFX) every time the narrative jumps forward in time to train the viewer's eye.
*   **Future projection ending:** End the video by projecting the current timeline into the future, leaving the audience with a humorous or daunting mathematical realization about their own aging.
*   **Shot length pacing:** Allow shots to breathe longer (up to 15 seconds average) when explaining complex lore or development timelines, relying on continuous on-screen action rather than rapid camera cuts.

## Hero hype
*   **Mid-action cold open:** Start the first frame mid-action with an aggressive, 100% energy voiceover line to immediately establish a chaotic tone before the viewer can process what they are watching.
*   **Persistent progress UI:** Keep a vertical ranking list or progress bar permanently visible on the edge of the screen, highlighting the current number to visually promise a climax and retain viewers.
*   **Clip duration limits:** Strictly limit individual compilation clips to a maximum of 7 seconds to maintain a relentless, fast-paced rhythm.
*   **Meme SFX punctuation:** Insert recognizable, exaggerated sound effects (e.g., retro gaming jumps, anime swooshes) exactly on the frames of physical impacts to turn mundane footage into comedic events.
*   **Climax hoarding:** Reserve the objectively most visually impressive or dramatic piece of footage exclusively for the final 10 to 15 seconds of the video.
*   **Hype filters:** Apply a sudden visual filter (like high-contrast black and white or deep color saturation) exactly at the moment of peak impact in the final clip.
*   **Music drop sync:** Keep background music minimal or absent early on, then trigger a heavy, bass-boosted music drop (e.g., 120 BPM trap/phonk) perfectly synced with the final visual climax.

## No words, just music
*   **Unbroken simulation:** Use a single, continuous 3D camera shot for the entire duration (e.g., 30+ seconds) to keep the viewer mesmerized by the physics or process without jarring cuts.
*   **Textual premise hook:** Place static, highly readable text on screen in the first 2 seconds that explicitly states the grand question or scenario being visualized.
*   **Scale indicators:** Include a running on-screen timer, measurement gauge, or recognizable object to ground abstract or massive concepts in a relatable reality.
*   **Dynamic color state changes:** Use the color palette to signify a massive physical transformation, transitioning from dull colors (greys/browns) to vibrant, glowing colors (oranges/yellows) exactly at the point of impact or chemical change.
*   **Minimalist tracking labels:** Use thin, static white lines and simple text to identify key elements moving within a chaotic visual field so the viewer doesn't get lost.
*   **Musical crescendo:** Structure the video around a cinematic orchestral track (approx. 60 BPM), ensuring the visual climax perfectly aligns with the music's maximum swell to replace the emotional weight of a voiceover.
*   **Final trivia reward:** Display one surprising, specific text fact in the final 5 seconds to reward viewers who watched the entire simulation and to close the narrative loop.
