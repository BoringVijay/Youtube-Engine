# Curious Penguin: creative bible

> Every Curious Penguin video should feel like a completely different creative experience, with the penguin quietly becoming the character people recognise, look for and genuinely look forward to spotting.

The director stage reads this file before planning any video. Change it here and every new video follows.

## Retired
- Stickman explainers and template infotainment (title card, stat card, bar chart, repeat). Never again. No stick figures anywhere, even as background extras: people are rendered in the video's universe (paper people, clay people, painted people).
- Same look twice in a row. Two consecutive videos never share a visual universe.

## The benchmark
The November 2026 lab: claymation boardroom with the shark staring at camera, the paper-diorama crane-down to the glowing cable, the blueprint with "minute taker (Aptenodytes forsteri)". Every frame has an idea in it, a clear focal point and a joke or a feeling. Nothing is generic.

## Pick the universe for the topic
Choose the medium that matches the topic's emotional truth, then commit to it for the whole video. One video, one universe. A second medium is allowed only as a deliberate device (see Nerd bonus).

Starting palette (not a limit, invent new ones):
- Claymation miniature set: absurd what-ifs, slapstick, cosy chaos
- Layered paper diorama: calm wonder, geography, oceans, history
- Tilt-shift miniature photography: cities, logistics, crowds, "the world as a toy"
- Macro photography of handmade sets: tiny mechanisms, food, insects, hidden details
- 16mm / Super 8 documentary still: real events, industry, "what actually happened"
- Film noir, high contrast black and white: heists, hacks, scams, mysteries
- Old master oil painting allegory: power, money, myths, big human stories
- Risograph or zine print: money, economics, culture, internet stuff
- Mixed-media meme collage: pop culture, movies, versus battles, trends
- Blueprint / technical cutaway: how things work, engineering, science
- Felt and wool, cardboard, puppet theatre, museum diorama: gentle, nostalgic, kid-at-heart topics
- Surreal (floating objects, impossible scale, dream logic): philosophy, scale, "what if" extremes
- Found footage, security cam, VHS infomercial parody: conspiracies, products, scams
- Nature documentary parody: corporations, crowds, internet behaviour treated like wildlife

Write a look bible for the chosen universe before any frame: medium, materials, palette (3 to 5 colours), lighting, lens, texture, camera language, typography for on-screen text. Every image prompt repeats the look bible so frames stay coherent.

## Cinematography
- Every shot has one idea and one focal point. Leave calm space in the lower fifth for captions.
- Vary shot size like a film: wide to establish, medium for action, close or macro for the detail that pays off.
- Camera language per moment: slow push-in for revelation, crane down for scale, locked-off for deadpan comedy, snap zoom or whip-pan for punchlines, handheld drift for tension.
- Rhythm: in the first 15 seconds change the picture every 2 to 3 seconds. Elsewhere 4 to 7 seconds, longer for emotional beats.
- Use match cuts and visual callbacks (a shape, prop or colour that returns later).
- Motion budget goes to the hook, the twist, the biggest joke and the emotional peak. Everything else is a designed still with a camera move.

## Humour
Deadpan and surreal beats loud. Background jokes reward re-watching (signs, labels, tiny props). Roast systems and ideas, never victims. No jokes on tragic beats.

## Nerd bonus
Every "nerd bonus" moment cuts to a blueprint / technical plate, whatever the universe. It is the channel's signature device, so viewers learn to expect it.

## The penguin
Fixed traits, never change them: small round egg-shaped Adelie-type penguin, bottom-heavy. Black head and back, white belly. Big round white eye-rings (permanently curious). Small dark beak with an orange tip. Slightly oversized bright orange feet. Flippers a bit too short, it cannot fly. Signature: three thin stubborn feathers sticking up from the top of its head, slightly bent. Nosy, deadpan, quietly unlucky. Never talks, never narrates.

The reference sheet lives at `creative/penguin-sheet.png`. Every frame with the penguin passes it as a reference.

Rendered in the video's universe: clay penguin in claymation, paper penguin in paper, linework penguin in blueprint, painted penguin in oil.

Cameos:
- Long video: 2 to 4 cameos. Short: 1 (sometimes 0, so people keep looking).
- Mix the types: hidden spot-it (small in frame), eavesdropper on an important conversation, caught in the middle of an event, slapstick victim (falls, trips, gets splashed), wrong place entirely, in an outfit that fits the scene (hard hat, toga, trench coat, lab coat).
- It is never the main character and never explains anything. Spotting it is the fun.
- Never during a tragic or serious beat. It can appear just after, quietly, as a small human moment.
- At least one cameo per video is subtle enough that most people miss it first time.
- Running gag: somewhere near the end it tries to fly. It always fails. Vary how.

## Words on screen
AI clips and images must not carry numbers or important text. Numbers, charts and labels are designed overlays drawn in the universe's style (clay numbers, paper cut-out digits, chalk, typewriter, blueprint labels). Keep on-screen text to a few words.

## Sound
- Voice: a friend telling you something wild on a terrace at night. Loud, warm, animated, real laughs and sighs, slows down for eerie or serious bits. Recorded in whole takes, not line by line.
- SFX that fit the medium (clay squish, paper rustle, projector click, typewriter, vinyl crackle). Meme-style cues sparingly, only where they genuinely land: a bass boom on a reveal, a record scratch, crickets, a sad trombone.
- Never rip trending audio from TikTok or Instagram (copyright claims take the revenue). Use original sounds, generated sounds or the YouTube Audio Library.
- Silence is a sound. Drop everything for the biggest line.

## Truth
Real numbers only, from the script's verify list. Speculation is framed as speculation. Fun never beats accuracy.
