## The big decision: how the picture is made

**Recommendation: build it in code, the way the reference is built. Don't make AI video the main motion.**

The teardown and the measured reports show the reference is a real-time 3D render:
- ink outlines on the geometry
- painted textures
- lens blur
- a colour grade
- flat cartoon cards lit by the scene
- symmetric camera glides

AI video can't hold flat 2D characters consistent across 15 shots, and it adds the "AI look" Vijay bans. So yes, this reverses my 14 Nov "flip the hybrid" idea, and it's the honest call. The Emu War pilot also showed the code look already works (Vijay: "visually good"). What failed was the story, the animation density and the rig glitches.

The fix for those failures is the bridge engine from the zip:
- **Characters are drawn in code**, not sliced from AI images:
  - bean heads, stick limbs and ink-dot hands, with one continuous outline
  - so no torn joints and no white gaps (the exact Emu War bugs)
  - any pose, 14 expressions, blinks every 0.5-2.3 s, bouncy planted walk cycles
  - characters about 3.5 heads tall, which fixes the "huge heads" note
- **The look reached measured scores** against 20 reference Shorts (camera, colour, ink, depth, textures).
- **Proven pieces:** crowds, impact kicks, foreground props, the DOF table, golden/day light presets, synthesized SFX and score, and frame-by-frame QA.

AI is used only where it beats code:
- **Nano Banana Pro** (Google credit) for the painted textures, the backdrop paintings, signs and posters, and one-off props.
- **Veo**: at most 1-2 effect shots, and only if code can't do them (probably none here).

## Voice: match the reference narrator
The reference narrator is male, fast and steady (median 3.7 words/s), with almost no pauses, heavily compressed and loud, and a deadpan delivery.

The plan is to measure, not guess:
1. Download 3-4 reference Shorts in GitHub Actions and cut 20 s of clean narration.
2. Generate the same sentence with 10-12 candidate ElevenLabs library voices, on v2 and v3.
3. Score each one by speaker similarity to the reference (a voice-embedding model) plus pace and pitch.
4. Vijay listens blind to the top 3 and picks.

Cost: about $0.30 on fal. If the winners are library voices fal can't load, that's the moment to buy the direct ElevenLabs plan.

The rule stands: never speed up the voice or cut its pauses. Write to length instead.

## Rules carried into this video (from Vijay, the rulebook, the teardown and the Emu War notes)
- **Template beats:** hook (an absurd act), doubters' quip, escalation with numbers, wait-what, "You see," plus an everyday analogy in its own mini-scene, an ironic payoff on the last word, and a loop.
- **Literal illustration:** every line is illustrated literally. Light shows time and mood (day → golden → night-blue for danger → warm interior for the reveal).
- **Scale:**
  - 1-2 epic wide shots per video. For this one: the ice-sheet aerial and the 2090 melt.
  - Crowds where the story needs them (200 soldiers digging).
  - Many actors animated at once, not one.
- **Impacts:** camera kicks that flip sign every frame. Music only where it lands (mock-epic stings, no constant funny music).
- **Background chaos** nobody acknowledges, with gags built for the second watch. Examples here: the frozen haircut, the melting ice sculpture, the lost penguin.
- **Penguin easter eggs:** one obvious, one subtle, premium-looking and with eyes. For example, a "Penguin Pizza" crate in the supply tunnel, plus a tiny penguin logo on a parka patch.
- **Cast and safety:** a globally appealing cast. Nothing that reads as AI. No real penguin IP and no ripped audio.
- **Politics:** Greenland is in the news. Tell it neutrally, with facts and dates only.
- **Spend:** checks against `config/budget.json` before any spend. Never paste keys in chat.
