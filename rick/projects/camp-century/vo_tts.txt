He shaved the walls of his bedroom every morning, because the walls were trying to eat him. You see, it's 1960 and Hank lives thirty feet under Greenland's ice in a secret army city with a movie theater, a barber shop and a nuclear reactor down the hall. Officially, they study snow. Unofficially, the army wants six hundred nuclear missiles on trains under this ice and nobody told Denmark, who owns it. So every night Hank marks the wall with a pencil and every morning the mark is lower and the sergeant laughs, "Son, you're just growing." Then the barber has to cut hair sitting down and the sergeant's office door won't open anymore. By spring the crew chops a hundred and twenty tons of wall a month and the missile tracks bend like spaghetti. Wait, how does solid ice move? Ever poured honey on a plate? It creeps, too slow to see, too strong to stop, and ice does the same under its own weight. So in 1966 the army takes the reactor and leaves everything else, the fuel, the sewage, the radioactive water, sure the snow will bury it forever. And it did, until scientists ran the numbers. As early as 2090 the ice above the camp melts faster than it snows. The glacier spent sixty years eating the city. Now it's spitting it back out.
