He shaved the walls of his bedroom every morning, because the walls were trying to eat him. You see, it's 1960, and Hank lives thirty feet under the ice in Greenland, in a secret army city with a movie theater, a barber shop, a chapel, and a nuclear reactor humming down the hall. Officially, they study snow. Unofficially, the army wants to hide six hundred nuclear missiles on trains under this ice, and nobody told Denmark, who owns the ice. So every night, Hank marks the wall with a pencil, every morning, the mark is lower, and the sergeant laughs, "Son, you're just growing." Then the barber has to cut hair sitting down, and the sergeant's office door won't open anymore. By spring, the crew is chopping a hundred and twenty tons of wall every month, and the missile tracks are bending like spaghetti. Wait, how does solid ice move? Ever poured honey on a plate? It creeps, too slow to see, too strong to stop, and ice does the same under its own weight. So in 1966, the army packs up the reactor, and leaves everything else, the fuel, the sewage, the radioactive water, sure the snow will bury it forever. And it did, until scientists ran the numbers. As early as 2090, the ice above the camp melts faster than it snows. The glacier spent sixty years eating the city, now it's going to spit it back out.
