Imagine you build the most valuable company on Earth. In a Denny's booth.
It's 1993. You're 30. You and two friends think computers should draw like video games.
Fun fact: as a teenager, you washed dishes at Denny's.
So you build your first chip. It flops.
Then a game company pays you to build their console chip. And you realize your design is wrong.
So you call them and say: hire someone else. Also, please still pay us. And they do.
You lay off half the company. Your motto becomes: we're 30 days from going out of business.
One last chip. It sells a million in four months. You live.
You call your next one the world's first GPU. Nobody knows what a GPU is.
Then you bet everything again. Let anyone use your chip for anything, not just games. Wall Street hates it.
2012. A few grad students teach an AI to see, using two of your gaming cards. It crushes everyone.
You didn't build a gaming company. You built the engine of AI.
So you hand-deliver the first AI supercomputer to a tiny lab called OpenAI.
Six years later, ChatGPT. Suddenly every company on Earth needs your chips.
You become the first company ever worth five trillion dollars. More than Japan's entire economy makes in a year.
Denny's even puts a plaque on your booth.
You've won. Every keynote, people cheer the jacket.
But then your phone buzzes.
"Jensen, you just went viral."
"Your affair was exposed."
