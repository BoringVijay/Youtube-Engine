In 1932, Australia sent the army to fight birds. And the birds won. Western Australia. Wheat farms. Then about twenty thousand emus walk in, and start eating everything. The farmers are ex-soldiers, so they ask for machine guns. And the government says... sure. So Major Meredith rolls out with two soldiers, two Lewis guns, and ten thousand rounds. First battle. The emus walk into range. Fire! Twelve birds go down. Then... the gun jams. Next, a thousand emus head straight for a dam. Perfect ambush. Fire! And they scatter. Every direction. Like they planned it. Six days in, two and a half thousand rounds are gone. Fifty dead emus. Maybe two hundred. Nobody agrees. You see, an emu can sprint at about fifty kilometres an hour. And when one gets hit, the rest don't bunch up. They split into tiny groups. There's nothing to aim at. So the army goes home. The farmers complain. The army comes back. Guns on trucks. Ambushes at every dam. One more month. Final report: nine thousand eight hundred and sixty bullets. Nine hundred and eighty-six emus. Exactly ten bullets per bird. The army lost no one. The emus kept the wheat. And that's how, in 1932, Australia sent the army to fight birds.
